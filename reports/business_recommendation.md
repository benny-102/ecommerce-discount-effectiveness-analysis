# Business Recommendation: First-Purchase Discount Strategy

## Decision

Reconsider blanket first-purchase discounts and evaluate a more targeted promotional strategy through a controlled experiment.

## Evidence

Discounted customers recorded an 83.23% repeat-purchase rate compared with 81.50% among non-discounted customers. The 1.73 percentage-point difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.1211). For the Day-90 eligible population, retention was 74.48% versus 72.16%; this 2.32 percentage-point difference was also not statistically significant (p ≈ 0.0806).

Average total revenue per acquired customer was lower in the discounted group (approximately ₺4,198.08 versus ₺4,453.14). Repeat revenue per acquired customer was also slightly lower (approximately ₺3,050.93 versus ₺3,123.55). Statistical comparisons did not establish significant revenue differences.

The discounted group incurred approximately ₺346,822.75 in first-purchase discount expenditure. A hypothetical gross-margin sensitivity analysis suggested that repeat-purchase gross profit could exceed this expenditure under the tested margins. However, this comparison does not estimate incremental profit caused by the discounts.

## Recommended action

1. Do not treat the observed retention difference as proof that discounts work.
2. Test targeted incentives for customer segments where there is a plausible retention opportunity.
3. Compare a discount group with a suitable control group using randomized assignment where feasible.
4. Measure repeat purchase, net revenue, product costs, contribution profit and discount expenditure over a defined follow-up period.
5. Scale the promotion only if the experiment demonstrates a positive incremental contribution after all relevant costs.

## Important caveats

The dataset is observational. Actual product costs, operating expenses and full acquisition costs are unavailable. Consequently, actual gross profit, customer lifetime value and discount ROI cannot be established from this dataset alone.
